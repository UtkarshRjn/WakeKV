CONTEXT
-------
Goal: come up with my own algorithmic idea in adaptive KV-cache management,

seq_len = one folder per word seen so far, head_dim = numbers inside a folder.

THE FIELD IN 8 SIMPLE TENSIONS (each one hides a research idea)
---------------------------------------------------------------
1. Folders are kept or tossed based on popularity.
   Rule today: workers opened it a lot recently -> keep; nobody opened it -> toss.
   Catch: "nobody opened it YET" doesn't mean nobody will need it tomorrow
   (why chatbots forget earlier conversation under compression).
   Think: can we guess FUTURE usefulness from anything besides past popularity?

2. Every folder has a label and contents — we only ever look at the label.
   Label (key) = used to find it; papers inside (value) = what actually gets
   used. All scoring is label-matching (attention). Two folders found equally
   often can contain a contract vs a sticky note.
   Think: shouldn't "how much is inside" also matter when deciding what to toss?

3. Everyone gets the same shelf space, but workers aren't equal.
   Lazy workers check only newest folders; star workers dig through everything.
   Adaptive methods give stars more space — but assigned ONCE, at the start.
   Think: what do we learn halfway through the job that would make us
   re-divide the shelves?

4. Tossing isn't the only option — there's a whole range.
   Per folder: keep / photocopy smaller (quantize) / staple into a similar
   folder (merge) / basement archive (offload to CPU) / shred (evict).
   Shredding is forever; the rest are softer. Each option is a separate
   research camp; almost nobody chooses per-folder among all five.
   Think: which folders deserve the shredder vs just the basement?

5. Popularity is measured by a quick glance, not a real count.
   We watch workers for a few minutes (small observation window) and treat
   the sample as truth. "Opened twice vs once" in that window may be luck.
   Think: how to rank folders knowing the counts are partly random noise?

6. Folders are stored in boxes of 16 — you can only reclaim whole boxes.
   vLLM frees space only when a box is COMPLETELY empty. Shred 90% of
   folders scattered evenly and ~81% of boxes still hold one survivor —
   almost no space freed. Papers brag about folders shredded, not boxes freed.
   Think: should we choose which folders to shred — or which boxes to empty?

7. Nobody reads what's written on the folders.
   All rules use statistics, but the text has obvious structure: thinking vs
   connector parts in reasoning, heavy repetition in long CoT, question vs
   background in chat. ThinKV (ICLR 2026) used this: kept "thinking" folders,
   squashed "connector" ones -> threw away 95% with almost no damage.
   Think: what other patterns in the text itself is everyone ignoring?

8. Small buildings have no room for error.
   Big models tolerate mistakes (workers keep overlapping notes). Tiny
   on-device models (GQA, few KV heads) have no backup copies. The popular
   shredding rules were only tested in big buildings.
   Think: which rules break first as the building shrinks?

MY IDEA (v0): DYNAMIC WORKER PRIORITY
-------------------------------------
"Can we change a worker's priority (lazy vs active) WHILE the job is
running, and re-divide shelf space dynamically?"

Assessment from Claude:
- The direction is real and validated — it's where the frontier is.
- Nearby existing work (must lit-check before committing):
  * Ada-KV, CAKE: input-dependent budgets, but decided essentially once
    (at prompt processing).
  * LAVa (EMNLP 2025 Findings): advertises "fully dynamic" head+layer
    budgets emerging from score comparisons at each compression event.
  * DynamicKV (needs verification): adjusts per-layer budgets during
    inference.
  Neighbors prove the direction is publishable; the job is to find what
  they DON'T do.

- THE CRUX (where the actual idea lives): redistribution is one-directional.
  If lazy worker H3 suddenly becomes active mid-job, his old folders were
  already shredded — giving him empty shelves doesn't bring them back.
  Dynamic re-allocation is easy in the "take space away" direction and
  nearly useless in the "give space back" direction, because eviction is
  irreversible (tension 3 colliding with tension 4).

- Three design questions whose answers ARE the algorithm:
  1. Early-warning signal: how to detect a lazy worker is ABOUT TO become
     active — before his folders are gone? (recent text? attention trend?
     task phase?)
  2. Demotion policy: what happens to a demoted worker's folders — shred
     (cheap, irreversible) or something softer (photocopy smaller /
     basement) so a later promotion can actually be honored?
     Note: dynamic-budget papers appear to all use shredding, making their
     "dynamism" quietly one-directional. Reversible demotion would be a
     different animal. (One possible answer among several — not the only one.)
  3. Re-division schedule: every word (expensive), every N words, or only
     on a detected change — and what counts as "something changed"?

DIRECTION 8 — EXPERIMENT SHAPE (Q: "shall I run tests on each model?")
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes — but a deliberate grid, not "every model":
- Methods: one representative per family (~5 total): StreamingLLM
  (position rule), H2O (cumulative popularity), SnapKV (query-aware
  glance), PyramidKV (fixed layer budgets), Ada-KV (adaptive head
  budgets). All in KVCache-Factory already.
- Models: a SIZE LADDER within one family isolates size from everything
  else: Qwen2.5 0.5B / 1.5B / 3B / 7B. Add Llama-3.2 1B / 3B (+ 3.1-8B
  anchor) for cross-family generality.
- Budgets: 100% / 50% / 25% / 10% cache.
- Eval: LongBench subset + one needle test. ~5 methods x ~6 models x
  4 budgets, each run cheap at small scale; single 24GB GPU, weeks.
- Headline plot: accuracy-vs-budget curve per model size. "Which algo
  fails first" = whose curve collapses earliest as the building shrinks.
  Even stronger finding if the METHOD RANKING inverts with scale.
- The step that makes it a paper, not a benchmark run: correlate the
  breakage with the mechanism — number of KV heads (GQA sharing), head
  redundancy/overlap. "Method X breaks below 3B BECAUSE only 4-8 KV
  heads leave no slack for per-head mistakes" is the publishable claim.
- Before any GPU time: 2-3 day literature check that no 2025-26 paper
  already did the small-model study. Then reproduce ONE published 7-8B
  number in KVCache-Factory to prove the harness works.

MY IDEA (v1): DYNAMIC WORKER PRIORITY + BASEMENT INSTEAD OF SHREDDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------
(Tension 3 x Tension 4. Supersedes v0 by answering its design question
#2: demotion = offload to CPU, not evict.)

One-line pitch: "Dynamic DuoAttention with an undo button" — head-level
priority that changes DURING decoding, in both directions, because a
demoted head's folders go to the basement (CPU) and can be brought back
when the head is promoted again.

Why the gap looks open (pre-lit-check):
- DuoAttention (2024): retrieval vs streaming head castes, but assigned
  OFFLINE, once, static during the run.
- ArkVale (NeurIPS 2024): evict-with-CPU-backup and recall — reversible,
  but treats all heads alike (no per-head priority).
- ShadowKV / InfiniGen: CPU offload + selective fetch per step — again
  head-uniform.
- Ada-KV / LAVa: per-head budgets, but demotion = shredder.
Intersection (head-adaptive + reversible two-tier) appears unclaimed.

The two hard problems that ARE the algorithm:
1. Basement is far away (PCIe ~50-100x slower than HBM). Need an
   EARLY-WARNING signal so promoted folders are prefetched and the
   transfer overlaps compute instead of stalling decode. Candidate
   signals: head attention entropy trending up, score drift over recent
   tokens, task-phase change in the text.
2. Trigger economics: re-evaluating priorities must cost less than it
   saves. Every token? Every N? Only on detected change — and what
   counts as change?

Cheapest first experiment (make-or-break, ~1 week, tiny compute):
Measure whether head importance actually shifts mid-generation ("do lazy
workers ever wake up?"), how often, on which tasks. If never: idea dies
cheaply. If yes: that measurement is the paper's Figure 1 AND the
early-warning signal.

LIT CHECK RESULTS FOR v1 (2026-07-06, four parallel agent sweeps)
=================================================================

VERDICT: The exact combination is UNCLAIMED as of July 2026, but the
gap is narrow and closing fast (three near-misses appeared Nov 2025 -
Jun 2026). It is a two-axis gap:
  - Decode-time head-wise budget reallocation EXISTS (ReasonAlloc) but
    is evict-only — demotion destroys KV; a re-grown budget cannot
    recover lost entries.
  - Reversible per-head CPU offload with recall EXISTS (FlexiCache,
    HeteroCache) but head roles/budgets are frozen offline or at
    prefill — no head is ever promoted/demoted during decoding.
  - Nobody does BOTH: online head-priority changes + offload-based
    reversible demotion.

READY-MADE FRAMING SENTENCE (from the sweep):
"Prior decode-time reallocation is evict-only (ReasonAlloc); prior
reversible head offloading is static-budget (FlexiCache, HeteroCache);
we make head priority both dynamic and reversible."

PREMISE STATUS: PROVEN — do not spend time measuring it.
"Retrieval Heads are Dynamic" (arXiv:2602.11162, ACL 2026) directly
measures head-set turnover across decode steps (adjacent-step Jaccard
~0.28-0.51). Cite it as the premise. Bonus: it shows hidden states
PREDICT future head roles -> candidate early-warning/prefetch signal.
Also Meta FAIR 2502.09647: heads swing local<->long-context per query,
predictable from local keys alone -> second candidate trigger signal.
(Downside: a pure "do heads drift?" measurement study is scooped.)

POSITIONING BURDEN (what reviewers will demand)
-----------------------------------------------
1. Show static head classification MEASURABLY FAILS somewhere:
   long reasoning traces, task-phase shifts, multi-turn — the settings
   where 2602.11162 says roles churn most. This attacks FlexiCache's
   core premise ("temporal stability is model-intrinsic").
2. Show reversible demotion BEATS evict-based reallocation
   (ReasonAlloc-style) at equal GPU-resident budget.
3. Explicit delta vs HARD-KV (budgets vs roles; reversibility).
4. Handle the GQA constraint (BaKlaVa): budgets are per KV-GROUP, not
   per query head — small models have only 4-8 KV groups.
5. PCIe economics: promotion latency must be hidden (prefetch via
   early-warning signal) or the method stalls decode.
